# Administering the offline IELTS Academic pack

Use one complete form per sitting. Give the candidate only `candidate.md`, `answer-sheet.md`, the local Writing visual, Listening MP3s and blank writing paper (or an empty local document). The examiner keeps `answers.md`, `examiner.md`, `mock.json` and the per-part audio JSON records, which contain the scripts. Do not give candidates the entire folder during a supervised sitting: its examiner files contain all keys and scripts.

## Listening

Use the matching complete track from [AUDIO.md](AUDIO.md), or `audio/listening-full.mp3` within the chosen exam. It plays all four parts once with instructions, 60-second previews, midpoint pauses in Parts 1–3 and final checking time. Start after the candidate has the paper and answer sheet ready. Do not pause, skip, replay or add a second checking period during a timed sitting. Individual part files are for separate-part practice. The recordings use synthetic English stock voices and this pack's chosen practice protocol, not an exact reproduction of any test-centre delivery mode.

Alternatively, arrange a separate competent English reader, ideally two readers for conversations. One reader may distinguish roles naturally without imitating accents. Rehearse unfamiliar words first; keep the written script out of the candidate's view. A self-reader should describe the activity as transcript practice. The following instructions are for administering the script yourself; the MP3s already contain these pauses:

1. Announce the part number, its topic and the question range. Give **60 seconds** to inspect the ten questions before each part.
2. Read all spoken turns once at a clear, natural conversational or lecture pace. Do not speak role names. Do not emphasise answers, repeat an answer as a hint, or paraphrase the supplied wording.
3. In Parts 1–3, the `---` marker follows the information for the first five questions. Pause **30 seconds** and say, “Now look at the next five questions.” Then continue. Do not speak the marker. There is no midpoint break in Part 4.
4. At the end of Parts 1–3, give **30 seconds** to check answers before the next preview. After Part 4, allow **two minutes** for final checking. The candidate should write directly on the response sheet throughout.
5. For live reading, aim for roughly **25–31 minutes**, depending on reader pace; do not slow speech unnaturally to force a particular runtime. Log the actual time if comparing repeat sittings. The MP3 durations are measured and listed in [AUDIO.md](AUDIO.md). No additional transfer period is included in this chosen protocol.

Keep a simple record of delivery errors. If a reader accidentally reveals an answer, omit that item from any comparison rather than claiming a standard 40-item performance. Paper and computer IELTS delivery details can differ; use the candidate's provider instructions for test-day timing.

## Reading and Writing

Allow **60 minutes** for Reading, including entering all 40 responses. No dictionary, translation tool, internet or extra transfer time. The passages are authored for non-specialists; no outside factual knowledge is required. For paragraph matching, the bank covers all A–H paragraphs, and letters may repeat. For heading matching, headings are used at most once within that group.

Allow **60 minutes** for Writing. Suggest 20 minutes for Task 1 and 40 for Task 2. Task 1 requires at least 150 words and Task 2 at least 250. Responses should be connected prose. The six SVGs have full data transcriptions in the paper, so an image-rendering problem does not remove essential information. For Task 1, candidates report the supplied visual; causes, forecasts and measurements not shown should not be invented.

## Speaking

Allow **11–14 minutes** overall. The examiner asks the questions from `examiner.md`; candidates see only the Part 2 cue card when it is time. Part 1: 4–5 minutes on familiar topics. Part 2: one minute of preparation, up to two minutes speaking, then one or two brief linked questions; allow 3–4 minutes in total. Part 3: 4–5 minutes of general discussion linked to the long turn.

Choose natural follow-ups within the time, rather than forcing all questions into an exact sequence. Invite reasons, comparisons or examples without supplying an answer or correcting language during the interview. No real identity document is needed for practice. Any personal audio recording requires the candidate's agreement and a separately arranged recording method.

## Marking

Give one raw mark for each correct Listening or Reading answer, zero for wrong or blank; report `/40` separately. Letter case is immaterial. Use the printed answer form: a letter, Roman numeral, TRUE/FALSE/NOT GIVEN, YES/NO/NOT GIVEN or a word/number. Completion answers must fit the sentence and the exact printed limit. Punctuation in times and currency can be normalised (for example, `6.30` for `6:30`); words that correctly express the same number are acceptable only when they fit the limit. Currency signs or units already printed before/after a blank need not be repeated. A normal hyphenated word counts as one word. Ordinary recognised British/American spelling variants may be accepted, but do not overlook a misspelling that produces a different word.

Every key includes an excerpt and a reason. NOT GIVEN means the text neither establishes nor contradicts the statement; it does not mean the statement seems unlikely. For writer-view questions, distinguish the writer's position from a view merely described and rejected. Completion answers are drawn from the text or spoken wording; numeric answers use conventional numerals when speech spells the number out.

Writing needs a qualified human assessment of task achievement/response, coherence/cohesion, vocabulary, and grammatical range/accuracy. Task 2 has twice Task 1's weight in IELTS assessment. The notes identify relevant content, not a prescribed essay or official band descriptor. Speaking needs assessment of fluency/coherence, vocabulary, grammatical range/accuracy and pronunciation from speech. Do not infer pronunciation from a transcript. This pack does not produce an official IELTS band or equated practice-band conversion.
