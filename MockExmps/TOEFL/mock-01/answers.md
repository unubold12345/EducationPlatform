**1-10 (Biology).** Pollination is the transfer of pollen from the male part of a flower to the female part. Many plants depend on insects, birds, or wind to carry pollen between flowers. When a bee visits a flower to collect nectar, pollen sticks to its body. The bee then leaves some of this pollen on the next flower it visits. Without pollinators, many of the fruits and vegetables that people eat could not be produced.

**11-20 (Economics).** In economics, opportunity cost is the value of the best alternative that is given up when a choice is made. Because time and money are limited, people cannot have everything they want. A student who spends an evening at work gives up several hours of study. Economists use this idea to explain why individuals, companies, and governments must compare the benefits of different options before acting.

**36-45 (Earth science).** The water cycle describes how water moves continuously between the oceans, the atmosphere, and the land. Energy from sunlight causes water in seas and lakes to evaporate and rise into the air. The rising vapor cools and forms clouds, which return water as rain or snow. Rivers and underground streams then carry the water back to the sea, and the process begins again.

**36-45 (Sociology).** Social norms are the unwritten rules that guide how people behave in a particular group or society. They tell members what is considered polite, acceptable, or offensive in everyday situations. Although most norms are never formally taught, children gradually learn them by watching adults. People who break a norm may not be punished by law, but they often face disapproval from those around them.

## Write for an Academic Discussion - model response (score 5)

> In my opinion, cities should not make public transportation free for everyone, although I agree with Claire that cost matters for some riders. As Andrew points out, price is rarely the main reason people avoid buses. When a system loses its ticket income, it depends completely on tax money, and in difficult years the service may be cut, which hurts the very people it was meant to help. A fairer solution would be to keep normal fares but offer free passes to students, older people, and residents with low incomes. In this way, the people who truly need help receive it, while the city still has enough funds to add routes and run buses more often. A reliable system will attract far more drivers than a free one that is slow and crowded.

## Take an Interview - model responses (score 5)

**8. Thank you for joining me today. To begin, please think about the last thing you read for pleasure, such as a book, a magazine, or an article online. What was it, and what did you like about it?** (event)

> The last thing I read for pleasure was a novel that my sister gave me for my birthday. It is a detective story set in a small town by the sea, and I finished it in about three days because I could not stop reading. What I liked most was the main character, an older police officer who notices small details that everyone else misses. The ending also surprised me, because the person who committed the crime was someone I never suspected. After I finished it, I immediately looked for another book by the same author.

**9. People read in different ways. Some prefer printed books, while others would rather read on a phone or a tablet. Which do you usually prefer, and why?** (life)

> I usually prefer printed books, especially when I am reading for a long time. First, my eyes get tired when I look at a screen for more than an hour, and I already use a computer all day for my studies. Second, with a paper book I am not interrupted by messages or notifications, so I can concentrate better. However, I have to admit that reading on my phone is convenient when I am traveling, because I can carry many books without any extra weight. So I use both, but paper is still my first choice.

**10. Now I'd like to hear your opinion. Some people believe that young people today read less than earlier generations did. Do you agree? Why or why not?** (opinion)

> I only partly agree with that idea. It is probably true that young people read fewer novels and newspapers than their parents did, because there are so many other kinds of entertainment now, such as videos and games. On the other hand, I think young people actually read a great deal every day. They read messages, articles, and posts online for hours. The real change is that the texts are much shorter, so people have less practice concentrating on one long piece of writing. In my view, the problem is not the amount of reading but the type of reading.

**11. Here is my last question. Do you think schools should require students to read a certain number of books every year? Why or why not?** (opinion)

> No, I don't think schools should set a fixed number of books for every student. When reading becomes a rule, many students treat it like a task to finish, and they may choose the shortest books just to reach the number. That does not make anyone love reading. I believe it is more effective to give students time to read during the school day and to let them choose books that match their interests. Teachers can still encourage them by recommending good titles and discussing them in class. If students enjoy what they read, they will naturally read more.
