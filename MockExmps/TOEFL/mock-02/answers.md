**1-10 (Biology).** Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants make their own food. Their leaves take in carbon dioxide from the air, and their roots absorb water from the soil. Inside each leaf, light energy turns these materials into sugar. During this process, the plant also releases oxygen into the air. Because animals eat plants and breathe oxygen, almost all life on Earth depends on photosynthesis.

**11-20 (Economics).** The law of supply and demand explains how prices are set in a market. When many people want an item that is hard to find, its price rises. In contrast, when supply is greater than demand, sellers lower their prices to attract buyers. Over time, these changes push the price toward a level at which the amount offered matches the amount people wish to buy.

**36-45 (Geology).** A volcano is an opening in the surface of the Earth. Deep below the ground, it is so hot that rock melts into a thick liquid called magma. Because magma is lighter than the rock around it, it slowly rises toward the surface. When it breaks through, the volcano erupts, and the liquid rock, now called lava, flows over the land. Over thousands of years, layers of cooled lava can build a tall mountain.

**36-45 (Psychology).** Confirmation bias is the tendency to favor information that supports what a person already believes. People notice and remember evidence that agrees with their opinions while ignoring facts that challenge them. For instance, a manager who expects a worker to fail may see only that person's mistakes. Because the bias usually operates unconsciously, researchers are trained to look deliberately for evidence that could prove their own ideas wrong.

## Take an Interview - model responses (score 5)

**8. Thank you for joining me today. To start, I'd like to hear about a typical night. What time do you usually go to bed and get up, and what do you do in the last hour before you sleep?** (life)

> On a normal weekday, I go to bed at around half past eleven and get up at seven, so I sleep for about seven and a half hours. In the last hour before bed, I try to follow the same routine. First, I prepare my bag and my clothes for the next morning so that I don't have to rush. Then I take a warm shower and read a few pages of a novel. I have to admit that I often look at my phone as well, even though I know it keeps me awake longer. On weekends, I go to bed much later and sleep until nine or ten.

**9. Some people do their best work early in the morning, while others feel more awake late at night. Which kind of person are you, and how does that affect the way you plan your day?** (life)

> I am definitely a morning person. My mind is clearest between about seven and eleven, so I plan my day around those hours. I do the tasks that need the most concentration, such as writing essays or solving math problems, before lunch. In the afternoon, I feel slower, so I use that time for easier things like answering messages, shopping, or going to the gym. By ten at night, I can hardly keep my eyes open, and anything I study then, I forget by the next day. My friends who are night owls think this is strange, but getting up early suits me.

**10. Now I'd like to hear your opinion. Some people say that students today do not get enough sleep because of smartphones and social media. Do you agree? Why or why not?** (opinion)

> I mostly agree. Many students I know take their phones to bed and plan to check one message, but then they keep watching videos for an hour. The content never ends, so it is hard to stop. Messages also arrive late at night, and people feel they must reply at once. However, I don't think phones are the only cause. Students also lose sleep because of homework, part-time jobs, and early classes. Even without a phone, a student who finishes work at eleven and has a class at eight will be tired. So phones make the problem worse, but they did not create it.

**11. Here is my last question. Some schools have moved the start of the school day to a later hour so that teenagers can sleep longer. Do you think more schools should do this? Why or why not?** (opinion)

> Yes, I think more schools should start later, at least for teenagers. When I was in high school, my first class began at half past seven, and most of us were half asleep. We did not learn much in that first hour, so the time was wasted. If classes began an hour later, students would be more awake, and I believe their grades and their mood would improve. Of course, there are practical problems. Parents who start work early may have difficulty taking their children to school, and sports practice would finish later in the evening. Still, I think these problems can be solved, and well-rested students are worth the effort.
