**1-10 (Geology).** Plate tectonics is the theory that Earth's outer shell is broken into large pieces called plates. These plates move very slowly, usually by only a few centimeters each year. Most earthquakes and volcanic eruptions happen along the boundaries between plates. When two plates collide, the land can be pushed upward to form mountain ranges such as the Himalayas. The theory also explains why the continents have changed position over millions of years.

**11-20 (Psychology).** Human memory is the ability to keep information and use it at a later time. Psychologists believe that memory works in three stages. First, the brain receives new information through the senses, such as sight and hearing. Next, it stores this information, sometimes for a few seconds and sometimes for a lifetime. Finally, the stored information is recalled when a person needs it, for example during an exam.

**36-45 (History).** The printing press is a machine that changed the way books were made in Europe. Before it was invented, people copied every book by hand, so books were rare and expensive. In the 1400s, Johannes Gutenberg built a press with small metal letters that could be used again. Printers could now make many copies of a page in a short time. As books became cheaper, more people learned to read, and new ideas traveled faster.

**36-45 (Sociology).** Urbanization is the process by which a growing share of a country's population comes to live in cities. It typically occurs when people leave rural areas in search of employment and education. Although cities offer greater opportunities, rapid growth frequently creates overcrowded housing and heavy traffic. Governments must therefore plan carefully for water, transport, and schools. Today, more than half of the world's people live in urban areas, and the proportion is still rising.

## Build a Sentence

One point for a sentence with every box in the right place; otherwise zero.

1. **Did you remember to add the list of sources?** (yes no question)
2. **I do not think that the buses are running tonight.** (negation)
3. **How much does a basket of strawberries cost there?** (wh question) Unused box: `do`.
4. **You can use the one in the student lounge.** (modal)
5. **The food was much better than I had expected.** (comparison)
6. **Have you already asked a professor for a reference letter?** (perfect) Unused box: `did you`.
7. **Do you know if the library opens before eight?** (embedded question)
8. **The equipment is being checked by a safety inspector.** (passive) Unused box: `are`.
9. **Yes, the tutor who taught my cousins has agreed to help me.** (relative clause)
10. **If I had known, I would have driven over to pick you up.** (conditional)

## Take an Interview - model responses (score 5)

**8. Thank you for taking part today. Let's start with something simple. Think about the last meal that you cooked or helped to cook. What was it, and who did you make it for?** (event)

> The last meal I cooked was a simple fried rice, which I made on Sunday evening for my two roommates. We had some rice left from the day before, so I added eggs, carrots, green onions, and a little soy sauce. It took only about fifteen minutes. I chose it because it is cheap and fast, and because I did not want to waste the leftover rice. My roommates said it was delicious, although I think they were just being polite, since I put in too much salt. Anyway, we ate together at the kitchen table and talked for an hour, which was the best part.

**9. Thanks. Now let's talk about your routine. Some people like to cook at home most days, while others prefer to eat out or order food. Which do you prefer, and why?** (life)

> I prefer cooking at home on most days. The main reason is money: as a student, I cannot afford to eat in restaurants very often, and a home-cooked meal costs less than half as much. Another reason is that I know exactly what is in my food, so I can use more vegetables and less oil. Cooking also helps me relax after a long day of classes. Of course, I still enjoy eating out with friends once or twice a month, especially when we want to try food from another country. But for everyday meals, my own kitchen is the better choice.

**10. Now I'd like to hear your opinion. Some people think that eating meals together as a family is less important now than it was in the past, because everyone is so busy. Do you agree with this view? Why or why not?** (opinion)

> No, I don't agree. I think eating together is more important now exactly because everyone is so busy. During the day, the people in a family are in different places, and in the evening they often look at their own screens. A shared meal may be the only time when they really talk to each other. In my family, dinner was the moment when my parents found out what was happening at school, and when my sister and I heard their stories from work. It does not have to happen every day, and the food does not have to be special. Even two or three meals a week can keep a family close.

**11. Here is my final question. Do you think schools should teach all students how to cook basic meals? Why or why not?** (opinion)

> Yes, I believe schools should teach every student to cook a few basic meals. Cooking is a life skill, just like managing money, and not all children can learn it at home, because many parents work long hours. Students who know how to prepare simple dishes will be able to eat well and save money when they live alone for the first time. Cooking classes are also a practical way to learn about other subjects, for example measuring in mathematics or how heat changes food in science. The lessons would not need much time. One hour a week for a single school year would probably be enough to give young people confidence in the kitchen.
