**1-10 (Ecology).** A food chain shows how energy passes from one living thing to another in an ecosystem. Green plants use sunlight to make their own food, so scientists call them producers. Rabbits and other animals that eat grass are the primary consumers. Foxes and other hunters eat these consumers in turn, and the energy moves one more step along the chain. When living things die, bacteria and fungi break them down and return nutrients to the soil.

**11-20 (History).** The Industrial Revolution was a period of rapid change that began in Britain in the late eighteenth century. For centuries, most goods had been made by hand in small workshops. Then engineers built machines that could spin thread and weave cloth far faster than any person. Steam engines soon powered this equipment, and production moved into large factories. As a result, millions of people left farms and villages to find work in growing cities.

Over many years, the snow is pressed into hard ice. When the ice becomes thick and heavy, it starts to slide downhill. As it moves, a glacier can cut deep valleys into the rock below it.

**36-45 (Political science).** Democracy is a system of government in which political power ultimately belongs to the people. In modern states, citizens rarely vote on laws directly; instead, they elect officials to decide for them. Regular elections give voters a chance to remove leaders who have lost public support. A democracy also depends on freedoms that let people criticize those in power, such as a free press. Without these protections, voting alone cannot guarantee that a government truly answers to the people it governs.

## Write for an Academic Discussion - model response (score 5)

> I do not think laptops and phones should be banned completely, but I would treat the two devices differently. Yuki is right that a screen showing a game pulls everyone's eyes toward it, and that problem is real. However, as Emeka says, many students take better notes on a laptop, and some need one to follow the slides. A total ban would punish them for other people's habits. My suggestion is a simple seating rule: students who use laptops sit in the back rows or along the sides, where their screens are not in front of anyone, and phones stay in bags unless the professor asks the class to use them, for example for a quick poll. A rule like this protects students who are easily distracted without taking a useful tool away from the others.

## Take an Interview - model responses (score 5)

**8. Thank you for joining me today. To start, please tell me about the last time you listened to music. Where were you, what were you listening to, and why did you choose it?** (event)

> The last time I listened to music was this morning on the bus to campus. The ride takes about twenty-five minutes, and I almost always put my headphones on as soon as I sit down. Today I listened to a playlist of quiet piano music, because I had a presentation in my first class and I wanted to feel calm. Usually I prefer pop songs with a strong beat, but when I am nervous, music without words helps me organize my thoughts. By the time I got off the bus, I felt much more relaxed and ready to speak.

**9. Some people like to have music playing while they study or work. Others need silence to concentrate. Which is true for you, and why?** (life)

> It depends on the task, but most of the time I need silence. When I read a difficult chapter or write an essay, songs with lyrics pull my attention away, because I start following the words instead of my own ideas. I have tried it many times, and I always work more slowly. However, for simple tasks, such as copying my notes or cleaning up my files, I enjoy having music on. It makes boring work feel shorter. So my rule is easy: silence for thinking and music for routine jobs.

**10. Now I'd like to hear your opinion. Some people say that wearing headphones in public places makes people less friendly and less aware of others. Do you agree or disagree? Why?** (opinion)

> I partly disagree. It is true that someone wearing headphones is less likely to start a conversation with a stranger, and sometimes people do not notice that another person needs to get past them. But I do not think this makes them unfriendly. On a crowded train, most people would not talk to each other anyway. Headphones simply give them a little private space in a noisy place, and that can make them calmer and more patient. The important thing is to stay aware, for example by keeping the volume low and taking the headphones off when someone speaks to you.

**11. Here is my last question. Do you think every school should require students to learn a musical instrument? Why or why not?** (opinion)

> I think schools should give every student the chance to learn an instrument, but I would not make it a requirement for many years. Learning an instrument teaches patience, because you improve only through regular practice, and playing in a group teaches students to listen to each other. For those reasons, one or two years of lessons in primary school would be valuable for everyone. After that, students should be free to choose. If teenagers are forced to continue with an instrument they dislike, the lessons become a punishment, and that time could be spent on art, sports, or another skill.
