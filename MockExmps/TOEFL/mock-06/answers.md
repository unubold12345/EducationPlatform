**1-10 (Oceanography).** Tides are the regular rise and fall of the sea along a coast. The Moon's gravity pulls ocean water toward it, and the water rises into a bulge. A second bulge forms on the opposite side of Earth. As Earth turns, most coasts pass through both bulges, so they have two high tides and two low tides each day. The Sun also plays a part, and tides are strongest when the Sun, Earth, and Moon form a line.

**11-20 (Business).** Advertising is any paid message that a company uses to tell the public about its products or services. Companies place advertisements on television, in newspapers, and on websites to reach as many people as possible. A successful advertisement catches attention and persuades consumers to choose one brand over another. Because advertising costs money, businesses study their customers carefully before deciding where and when a message should appear.

**36-45 (Biology).** Bird migration is the regular journey that many birds make each year between a summer home and a winter home. Before winter comes, they fly to warmer areas where food is easy to find. In spring, they return home to build nests and raise their young. Some birds travel thousands of kilometers and use the Sun, the stars, and Earth's magnetic field to find their way.

**36-45 (Anthropology).** Cultural diffusion is the process by which ideas, customs, and technologies spread from one society to another. It often occurs through trade, migration, and conquest, which bring different groups into contact. The numbers we use today, for example, were developed in India and reached Europe through the Arab world. Societies rarely copy what they borrow exactly; instead, they adapt new ideas to fit their own needs and traditions.

One point for a sentence with every box in the right place; otherwise zero.

2. **Are you planning to go with your sister?** (yes no question)
3. **What kind of work does she do there?** (wh question)
4. **It is much quieter than my old one.** (comparison)
5. **I wanted to get a seat near the front.** (infinitive)

> I think social media can do both, but in my experience it has made people more connected overall. Thabo's example of a family spread across several countries is convincing, and I would add another kind of connection that he did not mention: people with unusual interests or problems can now find others like them. ## Take an Interview - model responses (score 5)

**8. Thank you for joining me today. I'd like to begin with a recent experience. Please tell me about the last time you bought something that you were really pleased with. What was it, and why were you happy with it?** (event)

> A few weeks ago, I bought a secondhand bicycle, and I am still very pleased with it. I had been taking the bus to the university every day, which was slow and cost me quite a lot each month. I found the bike on a website where students sell things they no longer need, and the price was less than half of what a new one costs. The owner let me ride it around the block before I paid, so I knew that the brakes and the gears worked well. Now I get to class in fifteen minutes, I save money, and I get some exercise at the same time. It was easily my best purchase this year.

**9. Some people enjoy going to stores in person, while others would rather buy almost everything online. Which do you prefer, and why?** (life)

> I prefer shopping online for most things, mainly because it saves time. I have classes all day and a part-time job in the evening, so I can't easily get to the stores before they close. Online, I can compare prices from several sellers in a few minutes and read what other buyers thought of the product. That said, there are some things I always buy in person. Shoes are a good example, because sizes differ so much between brands, and I also like to choose my own fruit and vegetables. So I would say I do about three-quarters of my shopping online and the rest in real stores.

**10. Now I'd like to hear your opinion. Some people believe that discounts and sales encourage shoppers to spend more money than they had planned. Do you agree or disagree? Why?** (opinion)

> Yes, I mostly agree. When people see a sign that says fifty percent off, they focus on how much they are saving rather than on how much they are spending. I have done this myself. Last winter I went into a store for a pair of gloves and came out with a jacket as well, only because it was on sale. I didn't really need it, and I have worn it twice. Sales also create a feeling of hurry, because the offer ends soon, so shoppers don't take time to think. Of course, a sale is useful if you were already planning to buy that item. The trick, I think, is to make a list first and keep to it.

**11. Here is my last question. Do you think schools should teach students how to manage their money, for example how to plan a budget and compare prices? Why or why not?** (opinion)

> Yes, I strongly believe schools should teach this. Managing money is a skill that everyone needs, but many young people only learn it by making expensive mistakes. When I first lived away from home, I spent most of my monthly budget in two weeks because nobody had shown me how to plan. A short course could cover simple things, such as writing a budget, comparing prices, and understanding how interest on a loan works. Some people argue that parents should teach these things, but not every parent has the time or the knowledge. If schools include this, every student gets the same chance to start adult life with good habits.
