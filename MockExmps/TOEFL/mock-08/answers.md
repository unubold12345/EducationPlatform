**1-10 (Astronomy).** The solar system is made up of the Sun and everything that travels around it. The Sun's gravity holds each planet in a regular path called an orbit. The inner planets are small and rocky, while the outer ones consist mainly of gas and ice. Moons, asteroids, and comets also belong to the system. The Sun is by far its largest member and holds more than 99 percent of the total mass.

**11-20 (Marketing).** A brand is the name, design, and reputation that set one company's products apart from those of its competitors. A strong brand helps buyers identify a product quickly and trust its quality. Because loyal customers often pay more for a familiar name, companies work hard to build one. They advertise widely and register their names and logos as trademarks so that rivals cannot copy them.

**36-45 (Ecology).** Tropical rainforests are warm, wet forests found near the equator. It rains there almost every day, so plants grow fast all year. The tall trees form a roof of leaves that blocks most of the sunlight. Below this roof, the ground is dark and damp. **36-45 (Science studies).** The scientific method is the systematic process that researchers use to investigate questions about the natural world. It typically begins with an observation that leads researchers to propose a hypothesis. Next, they design an experiment and collect data that either support or contradict the hypothesis. If repeated experiments by independent scientists give the same result, the hypothesis gains support; if not, it must be revised or abandoned. In this way, scientific knowledge always remains open to correction.

## Take an Interview - model responses (score 5)

**8. Thank you for taking part today. Let's start with your own experience. Think about a skill or hobby that you started learning recently, or one that you learned in the past. What was it, and how did you go about learning it?** (event)

> A skill I started learning last year is cooking. When I moved into a shared apartment for university, I realized that I could only make instant noodles and fried eggs. So I asked my mother to send me three simple family recipes, and I practiced one of them every weekend. At first the results were terrible. I burned the rice twice, and my roommates laughed at me. But I also watched short videos online to see the techniques, and little by little my food improved. Now I can cook about ten dishes, and I sometimes make dinner for the whole apartment. It has saved me a lot of money, too.

**9. Thanks for sharing that. Some people like to learn a new skill by taking a class with a teacher, while others prefer to teach themselves with videos or books. Which way suits you better, and why?** (life)

> For most things, teaching myself suits me better. The main reason is flexibility. With online videos, I can study late at night or early in the morning, and I can repeat a difficult part as many times as I need without feeling embarrassed. It is also much cheaper than paying for a course. However, I think it depends on the skill. When I tried to learn the guitar alone, I developed bad habits with my left hand, and nobody was there to correct me. So for physical skills like music or sports, I would choose a teacher, at least at the beginning. For everything else, I prefer to learn on my own.

**10. Now I'd like to ask for your opinion. Some people say that it is much harder to learn a new skill as an adult than as a child. Do you agree or disagree? Why?** (opinion)

> I partly disagree. It is true that children seem to pick up some things, such as the pronunciation of a foreign language, more easily than adults. They also have more free time and are not afraid of making mistakes. But adults have advantages of their own. They know how they learn best, they can plan their practice, and they usually choose a skill because they really want it, not because their parents decided for them. My father started swimming lessons at fifty, and after one year he could swim a kilometer. So I think the real obstacle for adults is not ability. It is a lack of time and the fear of looking foolish.

**11. Here is my final question. Do you think employers should give their workers paid time to learn new skills, even skills that are not directly connected to their jobs? Why or why not?** (opinion)

> Yes, I think employers should do this, at least for a few hours each month. First, a skill that looks unrelated today may become useful tomorrow. For example, an accountant who studies photography might later help with the company's website. Second, learning something new keeps people curious and reduces boredom, and workers who feel that their company invests in them are less likely to leave. Of course, there must be limits. A small business cannot let its employees study all week, and the worker should explain what he or she wants to learn. But within reasonable limits, I believe paid learning time benefits both the worker and the employer.
