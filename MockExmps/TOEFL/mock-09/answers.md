**1-10 (Biology).** DNA is the molecule that stores the genetic instructions of living things. The molecule looks like a twisted ladder, and each step is made of two chemical bases. The sequence of bases acts as a code, which cells read in order to build proteins. Before a cell divides, it copies its DNA so that each new cell receives a full set of instructions. Because this sequence differs slightly between people, DNA can be used to identify individuals.

**11-20 (Earth science).** Weather and climate are related, but they are not the same thing. Weather is the condition of the atmosphere in one place over a short time. Climate is the average weather of a region, measured over thirty years or more. A desert can have a rainy day, and one cold winter does not prove that a region's climate has changed. Scientists therefore study records covering many decades before drawing conclusions about climate.

**36-45 (History).** The ancient Romans built a huge network of roads across their empire. The first roads were made for soldiers, but traders and messengers soon used them too. Each road was built in layers, with sand and gravel below and flat stones on top. The middle of the road was slightly higher than the edges, so rainwater ran off into ditches. Some modern highways in Europe still follow the routes that Roman engineers chose.

**36-45 (Behavioral economics).** In behavioral economics, a nudge is a small change in how choices are presented that steers people toward one option. A nudge works without banning any alternative or changing financial incentives. For example, when a company enrolls new employees in a pension plan automatically, most of them stay in it. When workers must sign up themselves, far fewer join, even though the plan is identical. Critics warn, however, that nudges can influence people without their noticing.

## Write for an Academic Discussion - model response (score 5)

> I believe that voting should remain voluntary, although I understand Nikolai's point that a higher turnout makes a result more representative. As Zainab says, a vote that is cast only to avoid a fine is not a real opinion, and it may even make the result less meaningful. I would add another reason: a law is only useful if it can be enforced fairly. Checking millions of citizens and collecting small fines costs money, and the people who are fined are often those who had the most difficulty reaching a polling place. Instead of punishing people, governments should remove the obstacles that keep them away. Election day could be a public holiday, registration could be automatic, and schools could teach students how elections work. If voting is convenient and people understand why it matters, most of them will take part without being forced.

## Take an Interview - model responses (score 5)

**8. Thanks for making time for this interview. First, I'd like to hear about your own experience. Please tell me about a time when you helped someone in your community or took part in a volunteer activity. What did you do, and how did you feel afterward?** (event)

> Last spring, I took part in a clean-up day along the river near my home. A local group organized it, and about thirty people came. We spent the morning picking up plastic bottles and old bags, and my job was to sort what could be recycled. It was harder than I expected, because it was hot and my back hurt after two hours. But afterward I felt proud. When I walked along the river the next week, the path looked completely different, and I knew I had helped a little. I also met two neighbors that I had never spoken to before, and we still greet each other.

**9. Some people like to volunteer as part of a large, organized group, while others prefer to help in small, informal ways, such as assisting a neighbor. Which do you prefer, and why?** (life)

> I prefer to help in small, informal ways. The main reason is my schedule. I study during the day and work some evenings, so I cannot promise to be somewhere every Saturday morning. But I can carry shopping for the older woman who lives next door, or help my friend's younger brother with his math homework when I have a free hour. I also like that I can see the result immediately and that I know the person I am helping. Large organizations do important work, of course, and maybe I will join one when my hours are more regular. For now, small things suit me better.

**10. Let's turn to your opinion now. It is sometimes said that volunteering mainly benefits the volunteer, for example because it looks good on a job application, rather than the community. Do you agree? Why or why not?** (opinion)

> I don't really agree. It is true that some people volunteer because they want something to write on a job application, and we should not pretend otherwise. But the community still benefits. If someone spends a Saturday serving meals, the people who eat those meals are helped, whatever the reason was. In addition, people often start for selfish reasons and continue for better ones. A friend of mine began tutoring children because her scholarship required it, and two years later she is still doing it because she enjoys it. So in my view, the motive matters much less than the work that actually gets done.

**11. Here is my last question. In many towns, volunteers do jobs such as cleaning parks or running the local library. Should work like this be left to volunteers, or should local governments pay people to do it? Why?** (opinion)

> I think it should be a mix of both. Basic services, like collecting trash and keeping parks safe, are the government's job, and people already pay taxes for them. If a town depends on volunteers for essential work, the richer neighborhoods, where people have more free time, will end up cleaner than the poorer ones, and that is not fair. However, volunteers can add something extra. For example, residents can plant flowers, paint a wall, or organize a street party, and these activities also help neighbors get to know each other. So the town should guarantee the basics and then support volunteers with tools and small grants instead of replacing them.
