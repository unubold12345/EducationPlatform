## Standards used

- [Official Listening format](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-listening): four recordings, ten questions per part, everyday then academic contexts, one playback. A realistic test should include paraphrase and competing information; harder does not mean every detail must be a trap.
- [Official Academic Reading format](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-reading): three texts, 40 questions, a 60-minute section and varied comprehension tasks. Paragraph matching uses the full A–H bank with letters reusable, rather than four arbitrarily chosen paragraph options.
- [Official Academic Writing format](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-writing): two tasks within 60 minutes, 150/250-word minimums, report selection/comparison and developed argument. Task 2 has twice the official weight. Our responses still require human marking.
- [Official Speaking format](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-speaking): familiar questions, a prepared long turn, then wider discussion. Familiarity of a topic is intentional; the quality of the candidate's language determines performance.
- [Official sample tasks](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/preparation-resources/sample-test-questions/academic-test) were used to compare the task demands. No official questions, passages or recordings were copied into the site.

## Limits

These are original practice forms, **not official or statistically equated IELTS tests**. No band prediction or calibrated difficulty claim follows from this review. Qualified IELTS-teacher review and trials with learners at known proficiency levels remain necessary. Synthetic voices do not reproduce the full official accent range or live interaction. Source/file alignment and browser playback are checked; a complete human pronunciation/listening review of the recordings remains outstanding. The listening bank still concentrates on completion and single-answer multiple choice; map labelling and larger matching banks would widen preparation further. Fixed Speaking prompts cannot reproduce an examiner's spontaneous follow-ups. The existing timed format retains a 30-minute Listening stage plus a 2-minute check stage; exact delivery conventions should be confirmed with the student's chosen test provider.
